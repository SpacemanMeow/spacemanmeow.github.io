A **[mood diary](/glossary/what-is-a-mood-tracker/)** helps you notice patterns in how you feel — not by writing essays, but by logging a feeling in ten seconds and letting the trends speak. **Meow Mood** is a **private mood tracker** for Android and iOS that makes logging gentle: tap an emotion, add context, and watch the charts. Below we compare the apps people actually install — **Meow Mood, Day One, Journey, Daylio, and Grid Diary** — across mood tracking, privacy, price, and platforms. The same shortlist is often searched as a **free mood tracker** or a **mood journal app**, so we compare both quick check-ins and long-form writing.

## What a mood diary should do

- **Fast logging** — pick an emotion in one tap, not a blank page.
- **Context tags** — note sleep, people, or weather without a paragraph.
- **Trends** — a calendar and charts that show when lows cluster.
- **Privacy** — offline-first, with data that stays yours.
- **Export** — PDF or text to reflect or share with a professional.
- **A nudge** — a daily reminder that builds the habit.

## How we picked the best mood tracker app

Ranking a **mood journal app** is less about feature count than about whether you still log on day 30. We used four checks on every app in the table:

1. **Time to log** — one tap for an emotion, with optional activity tags. If an entry needs a blank page, it is a journal, not a **free mood tracker** you will keep using.
2. **Free tier that lasts** — a **best free mood tracker** should let you keep logging and reviewing trends without a trial clock or a paywall on the calendar.
3. **Data you can take with you** — export and a local-first default; a cloud-only account is a real cost when the app changes its pricing.
4. **Charts that answer a question** — a calendar heatmap and a mood/activity breakdown, not just a streak number.

By those checks, Meow Mood is the strongest **mood tracker Android** pick for quick, private entries, while Day One wins if you want long-form journaling with photos and don't mind a subscription.

Meow Mood is a **cat-themed mood and diary app** for Android and iOS. Logging is one tap: choose an emotion, add optional tags or a short note, done.

**Strengths for mood tracking**
- **One-tap logging** — an emotion icon beats a blank text field on a bad day.
- **Fully offline** — it works with no signal and stores entries on your device.
- **No upload by default** — data stays local; backup goes only to your own Google Drive if you enable it.
- **Trend views** — calendars and charts surface when low moods cluster.
- **Lock** — a fingerprint or passcode keeps entries yours.
- **PDF export** — reflect or share with a professional.
- **Daily reminder** — the nudge that makes it a habit.

**Weaknesses for mood tracking**
- **Lighter rich media** — fewer photos/audio bells than Day One.
- **Advanced export (CSV) is VIP** — free users get PDF.

**Best for:** people who want a **free mood diary** that's quick, cute, and private.

Day One is the original, most-awarded digital journal — praised by *The New York Times* and *Wired* as a "completely private digital space." It syncs across Android, iOS, Mac, and web.

**Strengths for mood tracking**
- **Rich entries** — text, photos, video, audio, location, and weather in one note.
- **Templates and prompts** — structured starts when you're stuck.
- **Encrypted sync** — entries follow you across every device.
- **Mature ecosystem** — a decade of updates, the most polished journaling app.

**Weaknesses for mood tracking**
- **Free tier is limited** — caps on devices and photos; full use needs Silver or Gold.
- **Cloud sync by design** — entries live on Day One's servers; privacy depends on trusting the vendor.
- **Heavier to start** — a blank rich entry is more friction than a one-tap mood.

**Best for:** people who want a full-feature journal and accept a subscription plus cloud sync.

## How the others compare

- **Journey** is a travel-leaning journal with Drive-backed sync; great for trips, less focused on quick mood logging.
- **Daylio** logs mood as icons plus a note, free with a subscription for history — the closest pure mood-tracking cousin to Meow Mood.
- **Grid Diary** structures entries through daily questions; good if prompts help you write.

## Typical scenarios

- **A bad-day quick log** — on a rough evening, tap one emotion and a couple of tags; no paragraphs required, so the diary survives the days you'd otherwise skip.
- **Private by default** — entries stay on-device behind a fingerprint lock; on a shared family phone your thoughts never sync to a vendor cloud unless you choose Google Drive.
- **Spotting the pattern** — calendar and charts surface a 'Sunday blues' or a post-coffee spike, turning vague feelings into something you can act on.
- **Offline anywhere** — log on a plane or in a subway dead zone; nothing needs Wi-Fi to capture the moment.

## FAQ

**Is Meow Mood free?**
{:.faq-q}

The core — quick mood logging, history, charts, and offline use — is free to start; a few extras (like CSV export) sit behind an optional one-time VIP.

{:.faq-q}

Yes. You can log, view, and edit entries with no internet and no account; data stays on your device.

**Is my diary private?**
{:.faq-q}

Yes — entries are never uploaded to our servers; you back up only to your own Google Drive, and a fingerprint or passcode lock keeps the diary to yourself.

**Meow Mood or Day One — which should I pick?**
