## Floating Assistant, in detail

Floating Assistant bundles three tools into one overlay: an assistive-touch ball for navigation, a floating clock you can keep above other apps, and a floating system monitor showing CPU temperature, usage, RAM and battery with a one-tap boost.

### Strengths
- **Three tools in one overlay** — you don't need separate apps for buttons, a clock, and a monitor.
- **Floating clock** — keep the time visible above any app or game.
- **Live monitor** — real-time CPU temperature, usage, RAM and battery, ideal while gaming or charging.
- **One-tap boost** — free RAM quickly when the phone stutters.
- **Screenshot, flashlight, and quick app access** from the ball.
- **Privacy posture** — uses Accessibility only for the listed controls and does not collect personal or sensitive data.
- **Protects physical buttons** on large-screen phones.

### Weaknesses
- **Android only** — no iOS version.
- **Overlay takes some screen space** if you keep clock + monitor on.
- **Extra themes/modules** sit behind an optional unlock.
- More features mean a slightly busier setup than a bare ball.

### Best for
Power users and gamers who want buttons *and* a clock *and* live system stats from a single floating layer.

**Download Floating Assistant**

## Assistive Touch (Easy Touch), in detail

Assistive Touch is the familiar iOS-style floating ball for Android: virtual home, back, recent and volume buttons, plus lock, screenshot, flashlight and customizable gestures — the simplest way to replace worn hardware keys.

### Strengths
- **Familiar floating ball** with virtual home/back/recent/volume controls.
- **Quick toggles** — Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, flashlight, brightness and sound mode.
- **Customizable gestures** (double-tap, long-press) to trigger actions.
- **Themes** to personalize the ball's look.
- **Free to use**, easy to enable and hide.
- Great for **protecting physical buttons** or one-handed reach.

### Weaknesses
- **Ad-supported**, which can interrupt use.
- **No floating clock** — it's navigation only.
- **No live monitor** — no CPU/RAM/battery readout, no overheat awareness.
- **No one-tap boost or RAM management** in the core experience.
- **Privacy caveats** — ad-funded apps typically request broader permissions than a monitor-only tool.

### Best for
Users who simply want an iOS-like navigation ball to replace or supplement hardware keys.

**Download Assistive Touch (Easy Touch)**
- [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.easytouch.assistivetouch)

## How they compare where it matters

### Scope of the overlay
Floating Assistant is a toolbox: navigation, clock, and monitoring in one. Assistive Touch is a single-purpose navigation ball. If you only need buttons, the ball is lighter; if you want more from the overlay, Floating Assistant does more.

### Monitoring while gaming
This is the clearest gap. Floating Assistant's live CPU/RAM/battery monitor and one-tap boost help during long sessions; Assistive Touch offers no system visibility at all.

### Clock and glanceability
Floating Assistant keeps a clock above other apps — handy as a bedside or desk companion. Assistive Touch focuses on controls and omits a clock.

### Privacy and ads
Floating Assistant positions itself as not collecting personal data, with an ad-free core. Assistive Touch is free but ad-supported, which is worth weighing if you're privacy-conscious.

## Which should you choose

- **Choose Floating Assistant** if you want one overlay that handles navigation, a floating clock, and live system monitoring (great for gaming and older phones).
- **Choose Assistive Touch (Easy Touch)** if you just need a simple, free floating navigation ball to replace hardware keys.
- **For an all-in-one daily driver**, Floating Assistant covers more ground without forcing you into multiple apps.
