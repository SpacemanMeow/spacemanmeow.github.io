## CPU Monitor, in detail

CPU Monitor turns your status bar and home screen into a live dashboard. Its headline feature is **real-time CPU temperature and frequency** with an overheat alarm, so you get warned before thermal throttling kicks in during a long game or fast charge.

### Strengths
- **Live temperature with an overheat alarm** — the single feature CPU-Z cannot match; it warns you to close heavy apps and cool down.
- **Per-core frequency monitoring** — see exactly which cores are spinning up, not just an average.
- **Floating window and widgets** — a status-bar overlay plus CPU/RAM/battery widgets keep the numbers visible without opening the app.
- **One-tap boost** — quickly free RAM when the phone feels slow.
- **Battery doctor** — health, voltage and temperature curves for the battery.
- **Lightweight and free at the core**, with multiple themes.

### Weaknesses
- **Android only** — no iPhone or iPad version.
- **Accuracy depends on sensors** — not every device exposes a real temperature sensor, so some readings are estimated.
- **Less deep on static specs** — it shows live numbers but not a full component inventory (e.g., exact GPU model, screen panel).
- **Extra themes/history** sit behind an optional unlock.
- **No export** of a spec report.

### Best for
Gamers, emulation fans, and anyone who wants to *watch* heat and load in real time rather than read a spec list once.

## CPU-Z, in detail

CPU-Z is the Android port of the famous PC utility by CPUID. It opens to a clean, tabbed spec sheet: SoC name and architecture, GPU, memory and storage, battery, sensors, and even screen details. It's the app you reach for when you need to *know what's inside*.

### Strengths
- **Authoritative, detailed spec sheet** — SoC, GPU, memory type, storage, battery design capacity, and a long sensor list.
- **Trusted brand** with a no-nonsense, ad-light utility interface.
- **Completely free**, no subscription, no account.
- **Great for identification** — settle "which chip is this?" or "is this really UFS 3.1?" in seconds.
- **Sensor tab** exposes live values (including temperature) for a one-off check.

### Weaknesses
- **Static, not a monitor** — it shows a snapshot; it does not track temperature over time or alert you when things get hot.
- **No floating window, widgets, or overheat alarm** — you must open the app to see anything.
- **No one-tap boost or RAM management.**
- **Android only**, and the UI is pure utility (no theming).
- **No trends or history** — you can't see how temperature changed during a game.

### Best for
Developers, shoppers verifying a used phone, and anyone who needs a one-time, trustworthy hardware readout.

**Download CPU-Z**
- [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cpuid.cpu_z)

## How they compare where it matters

### Live monitoring vs a spec sheet
This is the heart of the difference. CPU Monitor is a *monitor*: open it (or use the floating window) and you see temperature climbing, cores ramping, and RAM filling — in real time. CPU-Z is a *reader*: it captures the state at the moment you open it and presents it as a tidy report. If your goal is "is my phone overheating?", only CPU Monitor answers it continuously.

### Overlays and widgets
CPU Monitor gives you a status-bar overlay, desktop floating window, and home widgets for CPU/RAM/battery — persistent visibility. CPU-Z shows nothing unless you launch it. For someone who wants a glanceable dashboard, that's decisive.

### Gaming and heat protection
While playing, CPU Monitor's overheat alarm and live frequency graph tell you when to back off. CPU-Z can show temperature once, but it can't watch a session. For gaming or heavy emulation, CPU Monitor is the practical pick.

### Depth of hardware detail
For raw identification, CPU-Z wins. It lists the exact SoC model, GPU, memory standard, storage type, and a fuller sensor inventory. CPU Monitor is lighter on static detail and stronger on live behavior.

### Price and platform
Both are free on Android; CPU Monitor adds an optional unlock for extra themes/history, while CPU-Z is free with no upsell. Neither is on iOS.

## Which should you choose

- **Choose CPU Monitor** if you want to watch temperature, frequency and RAM live, get an overheat warning while gaming, or keep a floating widget on your home screen.
- **Choose CPU-Z** if you need a trustworthy, detailed spec sheet of your device — chip, GPU, memory, sensors — and don't care about live tracking.
- **Power users often keep both**: CPU-Z for the occasional "what's in this phone?" check, and CPU Monitor for day-to-day thermal and load awareness.
