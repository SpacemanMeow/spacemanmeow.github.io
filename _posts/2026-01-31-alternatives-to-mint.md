Intuit **shut down Mint in March 2024** and folded its features into Credit Karma. If you are looking for **alternatives to Mint**, you are not alone — millions of former users want a **budget app** that tracks spending clearly, without being pushed toward credit products or forced into a cloud account they don't trust.

This guide compares **seven** apps on the same dimensions. Each was picked either for **high market share** or for a **distinct strength** that sets it apart. Every app below is covered with its real advantages, its genuine weaknesses, and how it performs on the core capabilities people actually care about — multi-account, sync, reimbursement, budgeting, statistics, assets/net worth, and multi-currency. Download links are at the end of each section.

## What people lost when Mint closed

- **Automatic transaction import** from linked bank and card accounts.
- **One dashboard** for net worth, bills, and subscriptions.
- **Free, simple category budgeting**.

The trade-off was that Mint monetized through referrals and eventually steered users toward Credit Karma's credit business. The replacements below keep the good parts and let you decide how much automation and cloud storage you actually want.

![YNAB screenshot 5: YNAB (You Need A Budget): the methodology leader — Best Mint Alternatives in 2026 (After Mint Shut Down)](/assets/images/blog/ynab_5.webp){: width="180" .normal }

YNAB is the best-known **zero-based budgeting** app: every dollar is "given a job" before you spend it, built on four simple rules. It has the largest dedicated budgeting community and the most education (workshops, guides, forums).

**Strengths**
- **Strongest budgeting method** — zero-based allocation beats simple category tracking for changing habits.
- **Bank sync via Plaid** — transactions import automatically across web, iOS, and Android.
- **Excellent reports and net-worth tracking**, plus goal planning.
- Mature, well-supported, cross-platform.

**Weaknesses**
- **No free tier** — 34-day trial only, then $14.99/mo or $109/yr.
- **Steep learning curve** — you must actively budget; it is a system, not a passive tracker.
- **No dedicated reimbursement flow**, and **multi-currency is weak** (USD-centric).
- Reports are good but less flexible than a spreadsheet for ad-hoc analysis.

**Best for:** users who want to change spending behavior with a proven method and will invest the setup time.

Meow Money Manager is a cat-themed expense tracker and budget app for **Android and iOS**. Its most distinctive trait is the **interface**: a cute, cartoon-style design with **400+ rich category icons** that make daily logging feel light rather than like office work. Beyond looks, it covers the practical bookkeeping basics.

**Strengths (from the product description)**
- **Beautiful, cartoon UI with 400+ custom icons** — the standout trait; logging is visually rewarding.
- **Multiple ledgers** — keep separate books (personal, travel, business) and view one or all at once.
- **Budget tracker & category budgets** — daily / monthly / yearly periods, with both a total budget and per-category budgets tracked together.
- **Instant chart statistics** — daily, monthly, and yearly spending reports.
- **Reimbursement management** — mark an expense as reimbursable (e.g. a business trip) and track its status anytime.
- **Assets management** — track cash, funds, stocks, cards, liabilities, and net assets.
- **Recurring records** — daily / weekly / monthly / yearly auto-repeat.
- **50+ currency symbols**, **dark mode**, and a **fingerprint lock**.
- **CSV export** (all records or a date range) for Excel, plus **iCloud / WebDav backup**.
- **No account required** — data stays on your device.

**Weaknesses (objective)**
- **No automatic bank sync** — entries are manual (or recurring). This aids privacy, but it means less "set and forget" than Mint.
- **Cloud sync is iCloud / WebDav**, not a dedicated cross-platform account, so switching between an Android phone and iPhone is less seamless than account-based apps.
- **Advanced features** (multiple ledgers, CSV export) require a one-time purchase; the free core covers everyday tracking.
- Smaller ecosystem and no built-in investment or net-worth aggregation.

**Best for:** people who want clear tracking without a cloud account, who value a friendly interface, and who don't mind light manual entry.

![Monarch Money screenshot 4: Monarch Money: the closest Mint successor — Best Mint Alternatives in 2026 (After Mint Shut Down)](/assets/images/blog/monarch_money_4.webp){: width="180" .normal }

Monarch is the app most often named the **direct Mint replacement**. It aggregates banks, cards, loans, investments, and even crypto and real estate into one dashboard, with collaborative sharing so a partner can edit the same budget.

**Strengths**
- **Closest to Mint's dashboard** — net worth, cash flow, and subscriptions in one view.
- **Broad auto-sync** including investments and property (via valuation).
- **Collaborative** — share and edit with a partner; both see live updates.
- Clean web + mobile experience and good goal/budget tools.

**Weaknesses**
- **No free tier** (trial only), about **$99.99/yr** (or $14.99/mo).
- **Relatively young** (founded 2021) — smaller community and fewer third-party guides than YNAB.
- Like Mint, it depends on **aggregation reliability**; a broken connection means manual cleanup.
- **Multi-currency is limited** and it is oriented to the US market.

**Best for:** former Mint users who want the same all-in-one dashboard and don't mind a paid subscription.

Rocket Money (formerly Truebill) is built around **finding and canceling unwanted subscriptions**, lowering bills through negotiation, and giving a simple spending overview. It is the strongest pick if recurring charges are your main pain point.

**Strengths**
- **Best-in-class subscription management** — detects, tracks, and helps cancel subs; can negotiate lower bills.
- **Free tier** available; auto account tracking and budgeting on top.
- Credit-score monitoring and overdraft-protection alerts (premium).
- Very popular download numbers, so support and stability are solid.

**Weaknesses**
- **Budgeting is basic** — no zero-based or envelope method; it tracks rather than plans.
- **Auto-categorization is decent but less customizable** than YNAB or Monarch.
- Premium pricing is **variable** (often ~$6–12/mo, billed annually; can feel high for what it does beyond subscription canceling).
- No reimbursement flow, weak net-worth/investment features.

**Best for:** people whose real problem is runaway subscriptions and bills, not hands-on budgeting.

## How to choose

- Want the **proven zero-based method** and will learn it → **YNAB**.
- Want **free, private, no account, multi-currency, with reimbursement** → **Meow Money Manager**.
- Want the **closest Mint dashboard** with investments → **Monarch Money**.
- Want to **kill subscriptions and lower bills** → **Rocket Money**.
- Want a **free net-worth / investment overview** → **Empower**.
- Want a quick **"can I spend this?"** answer → **PocketGuard**.
- Want **envelopes and shared household budgeting** → **Goodbudget**.

## Migrating away from Mint

1. **Export your data now** if you still can: transactions (CSV), accounts, and saved budgets.
2. **Pick a replacement** from the table above based on privacy vs. automation.
3. **Recreate your categories** — most apps let you rename defaults to match Mint's.
4. **Set budgets and recurring items** (rent, salary, subscriptions) so tracking is smooth going forward.
5. **Link the bank or stay manual** — if privacy matters, a manual app keeps your data on your device.
