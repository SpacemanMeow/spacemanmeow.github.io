## Definition

**Budget rollover** is the behavior of carrying a category's unspent balance (or overspend) from one period into the next, instead of resetting to zero each period. The same word means very different things across apps.

## Key ideas

- **Positive rolls vs zeroed:** YNAB keeps a positive Available as-is into next month but shows a negative as a grey 0 (cash overspend is deducted from Ready to Assign, credit overspend becomes card debt).
- **Both signs roll:** Monarch writes surplus and deficit into one formula `last rollover + this plan − this actual = next rollover`, so overspend directly lowers next month's cap.
- **Per-category toggle:** PocketGuard adds unspent to next month's same-category cap and subtracts overspend from it; you can switch rollover per budget.
- **Decided by fill:** Goodbudget's envelope balance is perpetual; whether leftover carries depends on Add vs Set fill.
- **No rollover:** Meow Money Manager and EveryDollar recompute each period and never auto-carry balances.

## Why it matters in a finance app

If you're saving for a payment six months out, "positive balance must roll" is a hard requirement — only some apps support it natively. If you prefer a clean sheet every month and don't want last month's overspend polluting this one, a zeroing semantics (YNAB, EveryDollar) or a window-recomputed limit budget is more relaxing. Matching the rollover behavior to your habit matters more than the feature existing on a checklist.
